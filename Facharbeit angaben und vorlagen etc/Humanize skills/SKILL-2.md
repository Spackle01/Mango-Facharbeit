# Human voice

Use this whenever you write something a person will send, post or hand in as their own. The target is text a careful reader can't pick out as machine-written: specific, uneven, opinionated, written for one channel and one reader.

## 1. Before writing

1. Pin down four things: who is "writing" it, who reads it, the channel (WhatsApp, email, school essay, LinkedIn, Reddit, Fiverr gig, portfolio), and the language. If one is unclear and it changes the text a lot, ask.
2. Get real material. Specific details are what make writing human. If the user gave none, ask for two or three: what actually happened, a number, a name, what they think about it. Never invent anecdotes, stats, quotes or sources to sound human. Made-up specifics are worse than none.
3. If there's a voice profile or writing samples from the user, follow them over everything below (section 6).

## 2. What the research says humans do differently

- Humans vary more. Sentence length, paragraph length and tone swing more than in model text. (Muñoz-Ortiz et al. 2024; "Human Variability vs. Machine Consistency", 2024)
- Humans write simpler syntax with fewer nouns. Instruction-tuned models write noun-heavy, dense prose full of participle clauses, passives and nominalizations, even when told to be casual. (Reinhart et al., PNAS 2025)
- Humans show more negative emotion and less forced positivity. (Muñoz-Ortiz et al. 2024)
- Humans are specific. Models slide toward generic statements that could sit in any text on the topic, and they inflate importance. (Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing)
- Models overuse a small set of style words (delve, intricate, pivotal, showcase, underscore...). (Kobak et al., Science Advances 2025)

## 3. Rules

### Say something
- Take a position. Say what you think, what annoyed you, what you're unsure about. No "on the one hand / on the other" unless you actually land somewhere.
- Cut any sentence that could be pasted into a different text on the same topic.
- Don't inflate. Things don't "stand as a testament", "play a pivotal role" or "mark a milestone". Say what happened.
- Don't cover every angle. People pick the one or two things they care about and skip the rest.
- Stop when you're done. No recap, no moral, no "the future looks bright", no "What do you think?" tacked on.

### Rhythm
- Mix sentence lengths hard. A four-word sentence. Then a longer one that keeps going because the thought needs the room and the writer didn't bother to split it. Fragments are fine.
- Make paragraphs unequal. One-line paragraphs are allowed.
- Don't default to three. Use two examples, or four, or one. Three only when there really are three.
- Start sentences with And, But, So when it's natural.
- Repeat the same noun instead of cycling synonyms ("the app ... the app", not "the app ... the platform ... the tool").

### Words
- Plain verbs: is, has, uses, gets, makes, tried. Not serves as, boasts, features, leverages, utilizes.
- Verbs over nouns: "we decided" not "the decision was made"; "because it's cheaper" not "due to cost considerations".
- Contractions in anything informal (don't, it's, I'd; German: hab, gibt's, 'ne, but only if the writer talks like that).
- Avoid, English: delve, tapestry, testament, intricate, meticulous, pivotal, realm, showcase, underscore, highlight (verb), crucial, vital, landscape (figurative), navigate (figurative), foster, leverage, robust, seamless, holistic, multifaceted, nuanced, comprehensive, elevate, empower, unlock, harness, embark, journey (figurative), bustling, vibrant, boast, garner, resonate, interplay, enhance, streamline, cutting-edge, game-changer, paramount, noteworthy, invaluable, ever-evolving, dynamic, synergy, spearhead, bolster, captivate, profound, beacon, myriad, plethora, nestled, renowned, transformative, groundbreaking, unwavering, additionally, furthermore, moreover, notably.
- Avoid, German: maßgeblich, vielfältig, nahtlos, ganzheitlich, facettenreich, Meilenstein, unterstreicht, Bereicherung, Mehrwert, essenziell, zudem, somit, letztendlich, gewährleisten, Herausforderungen (as filler), Potenzial, innovativ, spannend (as filler), zukunftsweisend, "ein Zeugnis für", "spielt eine entscheidende Rolle", "Darüber hinaus", "Des Weiteren", "In diesem Zusammenhang".
- One of these in a long text is fine. Clusters are the problem.

### Structures to avoid
- "It's not X, it's Y" / "not just X, but Y" / "Es geht nicht nur um X, sondern um Y".
- Tails like ", highlighting ...", ", underscoring ...", ", ensuring ...", ", reflecting ...".
- Warm-up openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "In an era of", "When it comes to", "Imagine...", "Let's dive in", "In der heutigen Zeit", "Tauchen wir ein".
- Reveal tricks: "The result? ...", "Here's the thing:", "The kicker:".
- Fake ranges: "from bustling markets to serene temples".
- Vague authority: "experts say", "studies show", "some argue" (name the source or drop it).
- Hedge filler: "It's important to note that", "It's worth mentioning", "Es ist wichtig zu beachten".
- "This + verb" openers: "This highlights...", "This ensures...", "Dies unterstreicht...".
- Paragraph template on repeat: topic sentence, two support sentences, mini-conclusion.
- Chatbot leftovers: "Certainly!", "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if...", "[Your Name]".

### Formatting
- Match the channel. An email is paragraphs. A chat message has no headings. An essay has no bullets unless the assignment wants them.
- No em dashes (—). Use a comma, period, colon or parentheses. In German, a spaced en dash ( – ) is normal, but only if the writer uses dashes at all.
- No bold-label bullets ("**Speed:** It's fast."), no emoji bullets or headers, no Title Case Headings, bold almost never.
- German text gets German quotes „so“ (or none), never "so".
- No markdown syntax in plain-text channels (email, WhatsApp, forms).

## 4. Channel defaults (when there's no voice profile)

| Channel | Feel |
|---|---|
| WhatsApp / Discord | short, lowercase ok, fragments, no sign-off, 1–3 lines per message |
| Email to teacher / boss / client | greeting, 2–4 short paragraphs, point in the first two lines, plain sign-off ("Viele Grüße", "Thanks,") |
| School essay (Deutsch / English) | full sentences, clear argument, own examples, no buzzwords, no headings unless required, conclusion adds a thought instead of repeating |
| LinkedIn / portfolio | first person, one concrete project detail beats three adjectives, no "I'm thrilled to announce" |
| Reddit / forum | direct, a bit blunt, personal experience first, no intro |
| Fiverr / product text | what you get, how fast, one proof point, no hype words |

## 5. Process

1. Notes first: list the actual points in plain words, in the order the writer would say them out loud.
2. Draft fast in the target voice.
3. Cut pass: delete the first sentence if it's warm-up and the last if it's a summary. Delete every sentence that adds no fact and no opinion.
4. Rhythm pass: if three sentences in a row have similar length, change one. If every paragraph looks the same size, merge or split one.
5. Tell pass: check against section 3, or run the ai-tell-audit skill if it's available.
6. Out-loud test: would this person actually send this? Any line that sounds like a press release gets rewritten the way they'd say it.

## 6. Matching a real person

This is what gets closest to "1:1 like a human": copy one specific human, not "humans in general".

When the user gives writing samples (best: 3+ texts they wrote without AI, 300+ words total, same channel as the target), build a profile and show it to them once:

```
VOICE PROFILE: <name>
Sentence length: avg ~N words, range N–N, fragments yes/no
Paragraphs: short/long, one-liners yes/no
Openers / greetings: ...
Sign-offs: ...
Punctuation: commas (heavy/light), "...", "!!", dashes, question marks
Capitalization: normal / lowercase in chat / nouns lowercase in German chat
Words they use a lot: ...
Filler / slang: ...
Language mixing: German + English words? which ones?
Typos: none / some (which kinds, e.g. missing commas, "dass/das")
Emoji: none / which ones / where
Formality: du / Sie, tone
How they argue: straight to the point / story first / lists
Never uses: ...
```

Apply it over all defaults above. Only copy imperfections that actually appear in the samples. Invented typos and forced slang are a tell of their own.

## 7. Honest limits

- Nothing guarantees a detector pass. Commercial detectors (GPTZero, Turnitin, Originality) are trained classifiers that get retrained, and scores differ by tool and length. What this skill reliably does: remove the tells human readers spot, and the flat, predictable style statistical detectors measure.
- Long, uniform texts get flagged most easily. Real details, the user's own opinions and their own final edit beat any prompt.
